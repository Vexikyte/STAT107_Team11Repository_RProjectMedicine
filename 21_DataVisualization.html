SECTION 1

Screening of Prolonged Pain or Stiffness, Frequencies, and Physical Measurements

The research question to answer is the following:

During the 2009-2010 NHANES survey cycle, among NHANES participants from ages 20-69, are reported pain regions associated with differences observed in spinal mobility measurements?

From what we learned from data cleaning, we hold categories of variables pertaining to different survey coverage concerns from the NHANES datasets. - Criteria Variables: Some variables were aimed to test for eligibility with further-symptom questions. - Pain Regions: Variables listing the reported locations participants were experiencing pain. - Symptom Descriptions: Variables reported from patients describing their symptoms, such as when they occurred and what may have influenced them. - Physical Examinations: Measurement variables collected from the NHANES survey such as lumbar flexion, chest expansion and others. - Demographic Characteristics and Others: Broad variables for subgroup analysis such as ethnicity, BMI, sex, age, height and weight.

To answer the research question, we’ll need to learn how we should we perceiving the data pertaining to pain and spinal mobility, as well as any caveats that may challenge existing medical assumptions related to the question.

For clarification with the measurement examinations:

Lumbar flexion is measuring the increase in distance between two lower-back marks when bending forward. Higher lumbar flexion is generally better.
Chest expansion is the difference between chest circumference at full inspiration and expiration when breathing. Higher chest expansion is generally better.
Occiput-to-wall is the distance between the back of the head and the wall when standing upright. Lower measurements are generally better.

Overall though, it is a considerable possibility that the boxplot results from these measurements may challenge the association of regional prolonged pain and stiffness with the results from physical examinations. If the groups seem to have similar boxplots, the argument could be made that association may be inconclusive.
